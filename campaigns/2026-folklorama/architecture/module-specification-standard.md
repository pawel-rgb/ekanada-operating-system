## Purpose

The purpose of this standard is to define the mandatory structure, documentation rules and quality expectations for all Module Specifications within the Folklorama 2026 Educational Program.

The standard ensures that Modules 01–08:

- follow a consistent architecture,
- serve distinct functions within the program,
- remain aligned with the Folklorama 2026 Educational Framework,
- maintain clear scope boundaries,
- support multiple age groups and Polish-language levels,
- connect Polish culture with the lived Canadian context,
- remain suitable for later educational material development,
- do not prematurely become lesson plans or production briefs,
- use consistent metadata, terminology and document control practices.

This standard governs the form and internal logic of Module Specifications.

It does not define the substantive content of individual modules. Each module must develop its own Big Idea, Essential Questions, Learning Outcomes, Core Topics, vocabulary and design requirements within the boundaries of the approved program architecture.

---

## Authority and Document Hierarchy

The governing hierarchy for the Folklorama 2026 Educational Program is:

```text
Folklorama 2026 Educational Framework
↓
Program Architecture
↓
Module Specification Standard
↓
Module 01–08 Specifications
↓
Future lesson, activity and material standards
↓
Future educational materials
```

The Folklorama 2026 Educational Framework defines:

- the purpose of the program,
- intended audiences,
- educational goals,
- thematic architecture,
- bilingual assumptions,
- program-wide success criteria.

The Program Architecture defines:

- the relationship between the eight modules,
- the program narrative,
- progression across ideas, questions, vocabulary, language and culture,
- system-level content and material layers.

The Module Specification Standard defines:

- the mandatory structure of each Module Specification,
- documentation rules,
- scope control rules,
- differentiation standards,
- accuracy and verification expectations,
- prohibited content,
- versioning and adoption requirements.

Each Module Specification defines:

- the educational role of one module,
- its scope,
- its Big Idea,
- its Essential Questions,
- its Learning Outcomes,
- its content boundaries,
- its vocabulary,
- its connections with Poland, Canada and the wider program,
- its future development constraints.

A Module Specification must not contradict a document above it in the hierarchy.

Where a conflict occurs:

1. the higher-authority document takes precedence,
2. the conflict must be recorded,
3. the lower-level document must be corrected or formally reviewed,
4. no silent reinterpretation is permitted.

A Module Specification must not receive a status that implies greater authority or stability than its parent document.

## Required Module Specification Structure

Every Module Specification must contain the following sections in this order:

1. Metadata
2. Purpose
3. Module Role in the Program
4. Scope Boundaries
5. Narrative Thread
6. Big Idea
7. Essential Questions
8. Learning Outcomes
9. Indicators of Successful Engagement
10. Core Topics
11. Vocabulary (PL / EN)
12. Cultural Connections
13. Canada Connections
14. Suggested Learning Experiences
15. Future Material Categories
16. Age Differentiation
17. Language Differentiation
18. Connections to Other Modules
19. Accuracy and Verification Requirements
20. Design Notes
21. Future Extensions
22. Source
23. Related Documents

Sections must not be omitted unless a formally approved exception is recorded.

A section may be concise where the module does not require extensive treatment, but its function must still be fulfilled.

Module Specifications must use consistent heading levels, terminology and section order.

## Section Requirements

### 1. Metadata

**Function:** Identifies the document, its place in the program, version, ownership, language, status and relationships with adjacent modules.

**Mandatory content:** A human-readable summary of the YAML front matter, including document title, document type, program, module number, English and Polish names, module role, version, status, owner, primary language, parent document, previous module, next module and last updated. It must state that YAML is authoritative.

**Must not include:** Conflicting values, informal notes, unresolved titles, speculative status or uncontrolled file-system details.

**Relationship:** Provides document control information only; it does not replace Purpose, Module Role or Connections to Other Modules.

**Editorial risks:** Inconsistent duplicate metadata, partial updates, inconsistent module names and working titles presented as approved.

### 2. Purpose

**Function:** Explains why the module exists and what educational need it serves.

**Mandatory content:** Primary purpose, function in the wider program, participant relevance, educational direction and general limits.

**Must not include:** Detailed lessons, long topic lists, implementation instructions, repeated scope tables or outcomes presented as purpose.

**Relationship:** Purpose explains why the module exists; Module Role defines structural function; Scope Boundaries define content ownership; Big Idea defines enduring understanding.

**Editorial risks:** Becoming a summary of the entire module, duplicating Scope Boundaries or describing activities rather than purpose.

### 3. Module Role in the Program

**Function:** Identifies the module’s architectural role in the eight-module sequence.

**Mandatory content:** A concise role label, explanation of that role, function in the overall learning journey and distinction from other modules.

**Must not include:** Full content scope, detailed transitions, Learning Outcomes or production requirements.

**Relationship:** Must align with Narrative Thread, Scope Boundaries and Connections to Other Modules.

**Editorial risks:** Using a topic instead of a role, assigning duplicate functions or defining the role too broadly.

### 4. Scope Boundaries

**Function:** Defines what the module owns, introduces without developing and does not cover.

**Mandatory content:** Three subsections: This Module Owns; This Module Introduces but Does Not Develop; This Module Does Not Cover. Relevant boundaries with adjacent modules must be identified.

**Must not include:** Complete lesson plans, instructional strategies, generic program-wide statements or future extensions.

**Relationship:** Governs Core Topics, Learning Outcomes, Vocabulary, Connections to Other Modules, Future Extensions and Design Notes.

**Editorial risks:** Listing the same item in multiple categories, claiming another module’s content, treating introduction as permission for extensive development or excluding so much that the module loses value.

### 5. Narrative Thread

**Function:** Explains what part of the shared story about Poland the module tells.

**Mandatory content:** The module’s narrative contribution, connection with the previous module where applicable, preparation for the next module and reinforcement of curiosity and continuity.

**Must not include:** Fictional scripts, character dialogue, lesson storytelling, activity sequences or new brand characters.

**Relationship:** Supports Big Idea, Essential Questions and Connections to Other Modules.

**Editorial risks:** Promotional copy, repetition of Purpose or narratives dependent on unapproved characters.

### 6. Big Idea

**Function:** Defines the most important enduring understanding of the module.

**Mandatory content:** One clear idea that goes beyond a topic, remains meaningful after facts are forgotten, works across age groups and supports multiple forms of engagement.

**Must not include:** Topic lists, slogans without educational meaning, activity objectives or competing Big Ideas.

**Relationship:** Essential Questions explore it, Learning Outcomes make it observable and Core Topics support it.

**Editorial risks:** Writing a topic rather than an idea, excessive abstraction or exceeding module scope.

### 7. Essential Questions

**Function:** Organizes inquiry and invites exploration of the Big Idea.

**Mandatory content:** Normally three to five open-ended questions that support multiple ages, encourage observation or reflection, connect with participant experience and do not assume Polish ancestry or language ability.

**Must not include:** Quiz questions, one-word answers, factual recall lists, required private disclosure or questions owned entirely by another module.

**Relationship:** Must emerge from the Big Idea, align with Learning Outcomes and remain within Scope Boundaries.

**Editorial risks:** Disguised test questions, excessive abstraction, too many questions or duplication without progression.

### 8. Learning Outcomes

**Function:** Defines what participants may know, understand, recognize, communicate or demonstrate after meaningful engagement.

**Mandatory content:** Five categories: Knowledge; Understanding; Language; Skills; Attitudes and Engagement. Outcomes should use observable or interpretable verbs.

**Must not include:** Activity instructions, time allocations, scores, formal curriculum standards unless approved or outcomes outside scope.

**Relationship:** Must align with Big Idea, Essential Questions, Core Topics, Vocabulary, Indicators of Successful Engagement and differentiation sections.

**Editorial risks:** Vague verbs, outcomes unsupported by content, confusion with activities or identical expectations across all participants.

### 9. Indicators of Successful Engagement

**Function:** Identifies broad, observable signs of meaningful participation.

**Mandatory content:** Indicators may include recognition, observation, questions, Polish word use, Poland–Canada connections, curiosity and readiness to continue. The section must state that indicators are not tests, formal assessment criteria or universal requirements.

**Must not include:** Rubrics, scores, pass/fail criteria, rankings, mandatory thresholds or diagnostic labels.

**Relationship:** Provides broad evidence related to Learning Outcomes but does not replace them.

**Editorial risks:** Turning indicators into assessment, rewarding only verbal participation or ignoring quiet engagement.

### 10. Core Topics

**Function:** Defines the content architecture of the module.

**Mandatory content:** Essential Content and, where appropriate, Optional Extensions. Essential Content must be necessary to support the Big Idea and Outcomes.

**Must not include:** Detailed activities, exhaustive catalogues, content owned by another module, material specifications or unrestricted optional content.

**Relationship:** Must follow Scope Boundaries and support Learning Outcomes. Longer-term ideas belong in Future Extensions.

**Editorial risks:** Encyclopedic overload, hidden requirements and repetition of exclusions.

### 11. Vocabulary (PL / EN)

**Function:** Defines the bilingual lexical range supporting the module’s concepts and participant-facing language.

**Mandatory content:** Core Vocabulary, Supporting Vocabulary and Extension Vocabulary. Each entry should include Polish, English and a usage note where needed.

**Must not include:** Unprioritized glossaries, decorative bilingualism, unrelated terms, unverified translations or unjustified advanced terminology.

**Relationship:** Must support Learning Outcomes, Core Topics, Language Differentiation and Cultural Connections.

**Editorial risks:** Assuming full equivalence, vocabulary overload and context-free culturally sensitive terms.

### 12. Cultural Connections

**Function:** Defines how Polish culture is represented within the module.

**Mandatory content:** Relevant cultural elements, links between tradition and contemporary life, stereotype risks, diversity and areas requiring verification or consultation.

**Must not include:** Unsupported generalizations, one tradition as universal, culture reduced to food, costume or symbols, exhaustive cultural history or another module’s content.

**Relationship:** Aligns with Big Idea, Scope Boundaries, Canada Connections, Accuracy and Verification Requirements and Design Notes.

**Editorial risks:** Folklorization, cultural uniformity, historical fixation and token representation.

### 13. Canada Connections

**Function:** Places the module within participants’ Canadian and Folklorama context.

**Mandatory content:** Relevant connections with multicultural Canada, Polish communities in Canada, varied relationships with Poland, respectful comparisons and access for participants without Polish heritage.

**Must not include:** Competitive ranking, ancestry assumptions, simplified migration history or claims of uniform Polish Canadian experience.

**Relationship:** Complements Cultural Connections and supports participant relevance.

**Editorial risks:** Treating Canada only as contrast, forcing disclosure or decorative Canadian context.

### 14. Suggested Learning Experiences

**Function:** Identifies categories of future learning experience.

**Mandatory content:** May include observation, conversation, storytelling, movement, sensory exploration, comparison, creative expression, collaborative discovery, language encounter, family connection and personal meaning-making.

**Must not include:** Exercise instructions, lesson sequences, exact timings, scripts, step-by-step procedures or completed activities.

**Relationship:** Supports Learning Outcomes without becoming implementation documentation.

**Editorial risks:** Hiding lesson plans, prescribing one method for all ages or confusing experience categories with material categories.

### 15. Future Material Categories

**Function:** Identifies types of educational materials that may be developed later.

**Mandatory content:** Materials for Children; Materials for Families; Environmental or Display Materials; Optional Digital Materials; Educator Materials. It must state that listing does not authorize production.

**Must not include:** Completed content, layouts, artwork instructions, teacher scripts, exercise wording or production schedules.

**Relationship:** Must remain subordinate to approved module scope and outcomes.

**Editorial risks:** Turning categories into product briefs or defining materials before delivery conditions are known.

### 16. Age Differentiation

**Function:** Defines how the same module changes in complexity, language, independence and reasoning across age groups.

**Mandatory content:** Ages 5–7, Ages 8–10 and Ages 11–13, each addressing developmental emphasis, communication, expected independence and conceptual level.

**Must not include:** Three separate curricula, lesson plans, rigid claims or assumptions of identical ability within an age group.

**Relationship:** Modifies access to Big Idea, Essential Questions, Learning Outcomes, Vocabulary and Suggested Learning Experiences without changing module identity.

**Editorial risks:** Infantilization, excessive abstraction, topic changes instead of complexity changes and fixed ability assumptions.

### 17. Language Differentiation

**Function:** Defines four points of entry into the same module.

**Mandatory content:** No Polish; Beginner; Intermediate; Advanced. It must explain access to shared concepts and treat No Polish as a valid entry point rather than a deficiency.

**Must not include:** Four separate programs, assumptions that language equals cultural knowledge, compulsory repetition, advanced speakers as automatic translators or deficit language.

**Relationship:** Aligns with Vocabulary, Learning Outcomes, Suggested Learning Experiences and Age Differentiation.

**Editorial risks:** Confusing language proficiency with intelligence or identity, excluding non-speakers or making Polish decorative.

### 18. Connections to Other Modules

**Function:** Explains how the module fits the eight-module sequence.

**Mandatory content:** What it receives from the previous module, develops, prepares for next, recurring concepts and boundaries preventing repetition. Module 01 states no previous module; Module 08 states no next module and explains synthesis.

**Must not include:** Specifications for another module, premature drafting, duplicated Scope Boundaries or assumptions that an unapproved future document already governs.

**Relationship:** Aligns with Narrative Thread and Scope Boundaries.

**Editorial risks:** Repetitive transitions, claimed ownership of another module’s content or circular dependencies.

### 19. Accuracy and Verification Requirements

**Function:** Identifies information requiring validation, expert review or consultation.

**Mandatory content:** Geography, history, language, demography, contemporary information, regional, ethnic, religious and linguistic communities, Poland–Canada migration history, source quality and consultation where relevant.

**Must not include:** Unverified facts, invented citations, vague source claims, assumptions that consultation replaces evidence or claims beyond scope.

**Relationship:** Governs factual and representational quality across all content sections.

**Editorial risks:** Treating all facts as stable, using outdated data, overgeneralizing or presenting contested interpretations as settled.

### 20. Design Notes

**Function:** Translates the specification into constraints and priorities for future authors.

**Mandatory content:** Design priorities, content discipline, accessibility, representation risks, bilingual design principles, encyclopedic-overload risks, balance between tradition and contemporary life and brand alignment.

**Must not include:** Layouts, detailed visual directions, scripts, production instructions, new brand characters or repetition of the entire specification.

**Relationship:** Synthesizes implementation implications without becoming implementation documentation.

**Editorial risks:** Becoming a general brand summary, duplicating Scope Boundaries or prescribing unsupported methods.

### 21. Future Extensions

**Function:** Records possible later directions outside required current scope.

**Mandatory content:** Each extension must remain optional, fit the Big Idea, require separate approval and identify additional review needs where relevant.

**Must not include:** Hidden mandatory content, current requirements, complete future lessons, production commitments or another module’s current scope.

**Relationship:** Must not weaken Scope Boundaries or expand current Learning Outcomes.

**Editorial risks:** Uncontrolled scope growth or treating extensions as approved.

### 22. Source

**Function:** Identifies documents and evidence that formally inform the specification.

**Mandatory content:** Applicable Educational Framework, Program Architecture, Module Specification Standard, approved program architecture, brand sources and module-specific authoritative sources where needed.

**Must not include:** Technical file names instead of official titles, invented governance documents, unapproved documents as authoritative or unlabeled future documents.

**Relationship:** Establishes authority and evidence; Related Documents provides navigation and context.

**Editorial risks:** Confusing sources and related documents or listing nonexistent material.

### 23. Related Documents

**Function:** Identifies documents that support, extend or connect with the specification.

**Mandatory content:** May include the Educational Framework, Program Architecture, adjacent Module Specifications, brand knowledge sources, applicable Governance documents and clearly labeled future documents.

**Must not include:** Nonexistent documents presented as current, speculative governance titles or documents with unclear authority.

**Relationship:** Provides navigation and context but does not create authority by itself.

**Editorial risks:** Implying approval through inclusion, mixing current and future documents without labels or inconsistent naming.

## Language Standard

English is the primary internal documentation language for all Module Specifications.

Mandatory bilingual elements include:

- module names,
- vocabulary,
- participant-facing terminology,
- phrases whose educational function depends on both Polish and English,
- culturally or linguistically significant terms where translation requires explanation.

Polish should be included when it has a clear language-learning, cultural, terminological or participant-facing function.

Unnecessary duplication of the full document in English and Polish is prohibited unless separately approved. Full duplication increases maintenance risk, can create conflicting versions and does not automatically improve accessibility.

Where a translated version is required, it should be managed as a controlled translation or separate approved publication.

## Scope Control Standard

### This Module Owns

Contains content for which the module has primary responsibility. Owned content defines the module’s distinct contribution, may be developed through Learning Outcomes and Core Topics and should not be substantially repeated elsewhere.

### This Module Introduces but Does Not Develop

Contains content necessary for orientation, continuity or preparation for a later module. Introduction does not authorize extensive treatment. The owning later module should be identified where applicable.

### This Module Does Not Cover

Contains content that belongs to another module or document layer, exceeds the purpose, requires unapproved delivery details or would create duplication or scope inflation.

To prevent overlap:

1. each major concept must have one primary owning module,
2. recurring concepts must show progression,
3. introductory treatment must remain limited,
4. vocabulary repetition must have a clear purpose,
5. adjacent modules must state boundary relationships,
6. outcomes must remain within ownership,
7. Optional Extensions must not override exclusions,
8. Future Extensions must not become undeclared current scope.

Where two modules appear to own the same content, resolve the issue at architecture level before further drafting.

## Learning Outcomes Standard

Every Module Specification must retain five categories:

- Knowledge,
- Understanding,
- Language,
- Skills,
- Attitudes and Engagement.

Outcomes must remain within scope, be observable or interpretable, support age and language differentiation, avoid prescribing exercises, avoid formal assessment language unless separately approved and reflect the Big Idea.

Learning Outcomes must not yet be divided into Core and Extended categories. That decision may be reconsidered after delivery model, participant contact time and implementation conditions are confirmed.

---

## Engagement Standard

Indicators of Successful Engagement are descriptive signs of meaningful participation.

They are not tests, formal assessment, grading criteria, rubrics, completion requirements or a checklist every participant must satisfy.

Engagement may be expressed through speech, gesture, movement, observation, selection, recognition, drawing, collaboration, listening, questions, word attempts, curiosity or choosing to continue.

Indicators must not privilege fluent speakers, highly verbal participants or participants already familiar with Polish culture.

---

## Age Differentiation Standard

All Module Specifications must differentiate for:

- Ages 5–7,
- Ages 8–10,
- Ages 11–13.

Age differentiation must adjust conceptual complexity, language complexity, explanation, independence, reasoning, support and degree of abstraction. It must not create three separate programs.

### Ages 5–7

Emphasize concrete experience, strong visual and sensory support, short communication, recognition, guided participation, simple observation, non-verbal response and limited abstraction.

### Ages 8–10

Emphasize organizing information, simple comparison, supported explanation, greater independence, collaborative reasoning and meaningful terminology.

### Ages 11–13

Emphasize interpretation, multiple perspectives, contemporary relevance, precise language, independent reasoning, stereotype recognition and age-respectful presentation.

Age categories are planning ranges, not fixed statements about ability.

---

## Language Differentiation Standard

All Module Specifications must define four Polish-language entry points.

### No Polish

Participants must have full access to essential cultural and conceptual meaning through English and non-linguistic supports. Polish is an invitation, not an entry requirement. No Polish must not be described as lack, failure, weakness, disadvantage or remediation need.

### Beginner

Support recognition, optional repetition, short phrases, basic greetings, word–meaning connections and simple contextual use.

### Intermediate

Support short connected statements, description, comparison, questions and answers and contextual vocabulary use.

### Advanced

Support precise language, interpretation, extended responses, cultural nuance, perspective-taking and discussion of complexity.

Language differentiation must not create four separate content tracks. Language level must not be treated as equivalent to intelligence, age, cultural knowledge, Polish identity or family connection. Advanced speakers must not automatically become translators, assistants or cultural representatives.

---

## Accuracy and Verification Standard

All Module Specifications must identify factual and interpretive content requiring validation.

### Geography

Verify maps, borders, city and region names, administrative status, physical geography, distances, neighbouring countries, time zones and travel information.

### History

Verify dates, events, terminology, institutional names, political boundaries, migration periods and claims about causation or significance. Sensitive or contested topics may require consultation.

### Language

Verify translations, pronunciation, usage context, forms of address, regional variation, minority and community language claims and statements about who uses a language and where.

### Demography

Verify population data, community size, migration data, religious affiliation, ethnic or linguistic statistics and social trends. Time-sensitive claims must include appropriate dates.

### Contemporary Information

Review present-day information close to publication, including institutions, public figures, technology, sport, social trends, population information, youth culture, organizations and public policy where relevant.

### Regional, Ethnic, Religious and Linguistic Communities

Use appropriate terminology, avoid tokenism, distinguish self-identification from external labels, avoid presenting one group as representative of all Poland, recognize diversity and obtain consultation where representation may be sensitive.

### Poland–Canada Migration History

Use reliable historical sources, distinguish migration periods, avoid one simplified narrative, use careful terminology and remain within approved scope.

### Source Quality

Preferred sources include official public institutions, recognized educational and cultural institutions, authoritative geographic references, peer-reviewed research, professionally edited historical works, established language references, reputable archives and verified Polish Canadian community sources.

Unsourced promotional pages, informal social media posts and unsupported summaries are insufficient for substantive claims.

### Cultural and Historical Consultation

Consider consultation when content represents a community, addresses identity, describes a tradition, discusses migration, religion or ethnicity, interprets symbols, presents contested history, risks stereotypes or describes contemporary social experience. Consultation does not replace source verification.

## Source and Related Documents Standard

Module Specifications must use official document titles rather than technical file names.

A document must not be presented as authoritative if it does not yet exist, has not been located, has not been approved, has an unresolved title or has unclear governance status.

Future documents may be listed only when clearly labeled as future documents. They do not govern current specifications.

Governance titles must match actual repository files. Where exact references remain unresolved, use:

```text
Applicable eKanada Governance documents — exact references to be resolved
```

The Source section identifies governing or evidentiary inputs. Related Documents identifies connected documentation for navigation or context. Inclusion in Related Documents does not create authority.

## Prohibited Content

Module Specifications must not contain:

- complete lessons,
- lesson plans,
- teaching scenarios,
- step-by-step instructions,
- worksheets,
- quizzes,
- games,
- completed activities,
- page layouts,
- visual designs,
- graphics,
- illustration briefs,
- detailed teacher scripts,
- production-ready educator instructions,
- unverified factual claims,
- invented citations,
- unsupported cultural generalizations,
- new brand characters,
- unapproved changes to the eight-module architecture.

Module Specifications may identify categories of possible future experiences and materials, but must not create those materials.

## Versioning and Status

Controlled statuses:

### Draft

An early working document that may be incomplete and has not entered formal review.

### Draft for Review

A sufficiently complete document submitted for structured review.

### Reference Candidate

A substantially accepted working reference that still depends on unresolved governance, parent-document or final-review conditions.

### Approved

A formally accepted and governing document within its scope.

### Superseded

A document replaced by a newer approved version. The replacement should be identified where possible.

### Archived

A document retained for record-keeping and no longer active.

A Module Specification must not receive a status implying greater authority than its parent document.

At minimum, editorial corrections must be recorded, structural revisions must change the version, substantive scope changes require review, approved documents must not be silently overwritten and superseded versions must remain traceable.

## Adoption Requirements

The Module Specification Standard may receive status `Approved` only after:

1. The Folklorama 2026 Educational Framework has a controlled status sufficient to serve as parent.
2. The Program Architecture and eight-module architecture are recorded in authoritative program documentation.
3. The required section order is reviewed and accepted.
4. Metadata fields and naming conventions are confirmed.
5. Controlled status vocabulary is confirmed against Governance.
6. The language standard is approved.
7. The scope-control model is tested against Module 01.
8. Age and language differentiation models are reviewed.
9. The Accuracy and Verification Standard is accepted.
10. Source and Related Documents rules align with actual repository documents.
11. Exact applicable Governance references are resolved or a temporary reference is formally accepted.
12. Module 01 is checked for compliance with the final standard.
13. No prohibited implementation-level content remains.
14. Formal approval is recorded according to eKanada Governance.

Until these conditions are met, the document remains:

```yaml
status: "Draft for Approval"
```
