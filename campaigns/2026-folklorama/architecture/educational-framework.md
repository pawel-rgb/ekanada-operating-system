## 1. Purpose of the Program

The Folklorama 2026 Educational Program enables children and families to discover Poland through active engagement with culture, language, identity and shared experience.

The program is not a presentation of isolated facts about Poland.

It is an invitation to explore Polish culture in a way that is:

- engaging,
- contemporary,
- inclusive,
- age-appropriate,
- accessible in the Canadian context,
- meaningful for participants with different relationships to Poland and the Polish language.

The program supports the eKanada mission by connecting education, culture, identity and community-building within one coherent system.

---

## 2. Intended Audiences

The program is designed as a multi-level educational system.

### Primary age groups

- children aged 5–7,
- children aged 8–10,
- children aged 11–13.

### Additional audiences

- parents and caregivers,
- teachers,
- facilitators,
- volunteers,
- participants with no knowledge of Polish,
- multilingual and multicultural families,
- participants with Polish, partial Polish or no Polish heritage.

### Polish-language entry points

- No Polish,
- Beginner,
- Intermediate,
- Advanced.

The **No Polish** entry point must never be framed as a deficit.

### English access

The program must remain fully accessible to participants who use English only.

Access to cultural meaning, participation and belonging must not depend on Polish-language proficiency.

---

## 3. Educational Goals

The program is intended to develop:

- curiosity about Poland,
- positive associations with the Polish language,
- cultural awareness,
- openness to diversity,
- interest in continued discovery,
- confidence in participating across different language levels,
- family and community connection through shared experiences,
- understanding of Poland as a living and contemporary country.

---

## 4. Competency Areas

The program develops several connected areas of competence.

### Language competencies

- vocabulary recognition and use,
- meaningful communication,
- listening and reading comprehension,
- confidence in encountering Polish,
- bilingual and multilingual awareness.

### Cultural competencies

- recognition of selected traditions and symbols,
- understanding of cultural variation,
- awareness of geography and history,
- interpretation of cultural practices,
- understanding of contemporary Poland.

### Social competencies

- cooperation,
- communication,
- empathy,
- respect,
- participation,
- openness to different identities and experiences.

### Cognitive competencies

- observation,
- inquiry,
- comparison,
- interpretation,
- creativity,
- problem-solving,
- asking meaningful questions.

### Practical and expressive competencies

- creating,
- experimenting,
- movement,
- presenting ideas,
- participating in shared experiences,
- expressing observations and interpretations.

These competency areas must be integrated rather than treated as separate program tracks.

## 5. Approved Eight-Module Architecture

The program consists of eight approved modules:

1. **Welcome to Poland / Poznajmy Polskę**
2. **Symbols & Identity / Symbole i tożsamość**
3. **Everyday Polish / Polski na co dzień**
4. **Celebrations & Traditions / Święta i tradycje**
5. **Music, Dance & Arts / Muzyka, taniec i sztuka**
6. **Taste of Poland / Smaki Polski**
7. **Explore Poland / Odkrywamy Polskę**
8. **Poland Today / Polska dziś**

The modules form the following educational progression:

```text
Meet
↓
Recognize
↓
Communicate
↓
Celebrate
↓
Create
↓
Experience
↓
Explore
↓
Understand contemporary Poland
```

The approved roles are:

| Module | Role |
|---|---|
| Module 01 | Entry and Orientation |
| Module 02 | Recognition and Identity |
| Module 03 | Language and Communication |
| Module 04 | Tradition and Celebration |
| Module 05 | Creative Culture |
| Module 06 | Sensory and Social Culture |
| Module 07 | Geographic Exploration |
| Module 08 | Contemporary Synthesis |

The module sequence must not be changed without architecture-level review.

## 7. Program Narrative

The participant moves through one coherent journey.

The program begins by introducing Poland as a real, living and diverse place.

Participants then learn how Poland can be recognized through official, cultural and personal symbols.

They encounter Polish as a language that can be heard, understood and used to connect with others.

They explore how people celebrate, remember and maintain traditions in different ways.

They experience culture through music, movement and visual expression.

They discover how food can connect place, family, memory and hospitality.

They explore Poland’s cities, landscapes, regions and communities.

The program concludes with contemporary Poland as a country that continues to create, change and participate in the wider world.

The program does not claim to provide a complete account of Poland.

It should leave participants with curiosity and a desire to continue discovering.

## 8. Language Framework

English is the primary internal documentation language.

Selected educational elements may be bilingual where bilinguality is educationally meaningful, especially:

- module names,
- vocabulary,
- participant-facing terminology,
- selected Polish expressions,
- culturally meaningful terms.

The program must not duplicate all internal documentation artificially in two languages.

### Polish

Polish used in the program should be:

- natural,
- contemporary,
- accurate,
- age-appropriate,
- meaningful in context,
- accessible at different proficiency levels.

### English

English should:

- provide full access to essential meaning,
- support participants and families who do not use Polish,
- explain cultural concepts where needed,
- enable shared participation,
- avoid treating Polish as a prerequisite for belonging.

Equivalent educational experience is more important than literal line-by-line translation.

Language and culture must remain connected.

Polish should be used as a means of recognition, communication, participation and cultural understanding rather than as an isolated grammar or memorization exercise.

---

## 9. Age Differentiation

The program uses three age ranges:

- 5–7,
- 8–10,
- 11–13.

These are not three separate curricula.

All age groups should encounter the same module identity, Big Idea and broad cultural purpose.

Differentiation may vary:

- complexity,
- abstraction,
- independence,
- language demands,
- reasoning,
- expected depth of interpretation,
- form of participation.

Age differentiation must not fragment the program into unrelated versions.

---

## 10. Language Differentiation

The program uses four Polish-language entry points:

- No Polish,
- Beginner,
- Intermediate,
- Advanced.

Participants at different entry points may engage with the same concept in different ways.

### No Polish

Participants must have full access to:

- cultural meaning,
- visual and contextual recognition,
- discussion,
- participation,
- optional encounters with Polish.

### Beginner

Participants may use:

- single words,
- short expressions,
- supported responses,
- repeated functional language.

### Intermediate

Participants may use:

- short descriptions,
- simple comparisons,
- questions,
- connected statements.

### Advanced

Participants may use:

- precise description,
- explanation,
- interpretation,
- discussion of complexity and perspective.

Advanced speakers should not automatically be assigned the role of translator.

Language level must not be treated as equivalent to intelligence, cultural knowledge, identity or commitment.

---

## 11. Role of the Facilitator

The facilitator acts as a guide rather than only as a transmitter of information.

The facilitator should:

- encourage discovery,
- support participation,
- ask open and meaningful questions,
- adapt complexity without changing the module’s central purpose,
- create a safe and inclusive learning environment,
- support different language entry points,
- avoid presenting one family, regional or cultural practice as universal,
- distinguish verified information from personal experience,
- encourage curiosity rather than factual overload.

Detailed teacher scripts and lesson instructions belong to future lesson and teacher-guide layers, not to this Framework.

---

## 12. Role of Families

Families are partners in the educational process.

The program may encourage:

- shared discovery,
- conversation,
- optional use of Polish,
- intergenerational exchange,
- participation in cultural activities,
- continued exploration after Folklorama.

Family participation must not assume:

- Polish heritage,
- Polish-language fluency,
- access to specific family stories,
- agreement on one form of cultural identity,
- willingness to disclose private experiences.

Family engagement should remain invitational and accessible.

---

## 13. Relationship to Canada

Canada is not an add-on or decorative comparison.

It is the participant’s living context and should help make the program relevant.

Across the program, Canada may function as:

- a starting point for locating Poland,
- a context for multilingualism,
- a place of multiple and layered identities,
- a place where traditions travel and change,
- a setting for cultural exchange,
- a comparative geographic context,
- part of an ongoing Poland–Canada relationship.

Comparison must support understanding and must not rank countries, cultures or communities.

---

## 14. Cultural Representation Principles

The program should present Poland as:

- living,
- diverse,
- contemporary,
- historically informed,
- internally varied,
- connected to Europe and the wider world,
- experienced differently by different people.

The program must avoid reducing Poland to:

- food,
- costumes,
- national colours,
- folklore alone,
- rural imagery,
- famous historical figures,
- a single religious or family tradition,
- one regional experience.

Tradition and contemporary life should remain connected.

Claims about identity, community or cultural practice must avoid suggesting that every Polish person or family shares the same experience.

## 16. Accuracy and Verification Principles

Program content must distinguish between:

- stable information,
- changing contemporary information,
- interpretation,
- personal or community experience.

Special care is required for:

- geography,
- history,
- language,
- demography,
- migration,
- contemporary Poland,
- regional practices,
- communities,
- traditions,
- identity.

Authors and reviewers should:

- use reliable sources,
- verify contemporary claims near publication,
- avoid unsupported generalizations,
- identify uncertainty,
- consult appropriate language, cultural, historical or community expertise when required,
- preserve source records for review.

No document or material may introduce unverified claims merely because they are common, familiar or visually attractive.

---

## 17. Program-Level Success Criteria

The program may be considered successful when participants:

- engage actively in age-appropriate ways,
- develop curiosity about Poland,
- recognize selected cultural and geographic reference points,
- encounter or use Polish without unnecessary language pressure,
- understand that cultural practices may vary,
- connect Poland with contemporary life,
- participate across different language levels,
- express interest in further discovery.

Additional indicators include:

- families can understand and access the program,
- facilitators can adapt delivery without changing core intent,
- modules remain coherent and non-duplicative,
- materials are accurate and culturally responsible,
- the program can be reused and developed in future editions.

These criteria are program-level indicators and are not formal testing requirements.

## 18. Governance and Authority

This Framework is the highest educational authority within the Folklorama 2026 program documentation hierarchy.

The relationship is:

```text
Folklorama 2026 Educational Framework
↓
Folklorama 2026 Program Architecture
↓
Module Specification Standard v1.0
↓
Module 01–08 Specifications
↓
Future lesson-level standards and specifications
↓
Educational Materials
```

The Framework is authoritative for:

- program purpose,
- audiences,
- educational goals,
- competency areas,
- language access,
- family and facilitator roles,
- program-level success.

It does not define the detailed content of individual modules.

A lower-level document must not override or silently reinterpret this Framework.

Where an implementation issue suggests that the Framework itself requires revision, the issue must be escalated through Governance.

## 19. Scope Boundaries

This Framework does not contain:

- complete Module Specifications,
- lessons,
- lesson plans,
- activities,
- worksheets,
- quizzes,
- games,
- graphics,
- layouts,
- teacher scripts,
- production instructions,
- detailed vocabulary lists,
- detailed historical or geographic content.

It establishes the educational foundation and program-wide rules from which those later layers may be developed.

## 20. Future Development

After approval, development should proceed through the existing architecture:

1. approve the Program Architecture,
2. approve the Module Specification Standard,
3. complete and approve Module Specifications 01–08,
4. create a lesson-level standard,
5. develop Lesson Specifications,
6. create educational materials from approved lesson requirements,
7. conduct content, brand, language, accessibility and production review.

Future development after Folklorama may include:

- extended classroom versions,
- family learning pathways,
- community programming,
- digital adaptations,
- expanded age pathways,
- heritage-language enrichment,
- regional and community extensions.

Such development must preserve the approved module architecture unless a formal architecture-level revision is authorized.
