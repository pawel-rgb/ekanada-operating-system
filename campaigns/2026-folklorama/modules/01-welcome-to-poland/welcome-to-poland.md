## Purpose

Module 01 introduces Poland as a real, diverse and contemporary place that children can begin to explore through geography, people, language, culture and everyday life.

Its primary purpose is to create a welcoming first encounter with Poland and establish the curiosity required for the remaining seven modules.

The module should help participants understand that Poland is:

- a country in Europe,
- a place where people live, learn, work, play and create,
- connected to many families and communities in Canada,
- shaped by both history and contemporary life,
- open to exploration regardless of a participant’s ancestry or knowledge of Polish.

The module gives participants enough shared orientation to engage meaningfully with the rest of the program. It does not attempt to provide a complete overview of Poland.

---

## Module Role in the Program

**Entry and Orientation**

Module 01 serves as the shared point of entry into the Folklorama 2026 Educational Program.

It provides the minimum common orientation needed by participants before they move into more focused areas such as symbols, language, traditions, arts, food, geography and contemporary Poland.

The module should make the program accessible to participants who:

- have extensive knowledge of Poland,
- have limited knowledge of Poland,
- have family or community connections with Poland,
- have no Polish background,
- speak Polish at different levels,
- do not speak Polish.

Its role is not to summarize the entire program. Its role is to establish a welcoming starting point, a basic frame of reference and a reason to continue exploring.

---

## Scope Boundaries

### This Module Owns

Module 01 is responsible for:

- introducing Poland as the central subject of the program,
- identifying Poland as a country in Europe,
- introducing Warsaw as the capital of Poland,
- presenting Poland as a place where people live contemporary everyday lives,
- establishing a basic geographical relationship between Poland, Europe and Canada,
- introducing the idea that people may have different kinds of connections with Poland,
- creating the first meaningful encounter with selected Polish words,
- establishing curiosity and inquiry as the main approach to the program,
- preparing participants to continue into Module 02.

### This Module Introduces but Does Not Develop

Module 01 may introduce the following ideas only at an orientation level:

- language as part of cultural experience,
- identity and belonging,
- national and cultural recognition,
- cities, towns, villages and natural environments,
- migration and Polish communities in Canada,
- multiculturalism,
- tradition and contemporary life,
- regional diversity,
- similarities and differences between Poland and Canada.

These ideas are introduced only when they support orientation. Their fuller development belongs to later modules or future supporting documents.

### This Module Does Not Cover

Module 01 does not provide:

- a detailed history of Poland,
- a survey of Polish historical periods,
- formal geography instruction,
- detailed treatment of Polish regions,
- extended study of the national flag, emblem, anthem or other symbols,
- structured Polish-language instruction,
- grammar teaching,
- detailed study of holidays or traditions,
- detailed treatment of music, dance, visual arts or craft,
- detailed exploration of Polish food,
- a catalogue of famous people, achievements or inventions,
- a comprehensive account of Polish migration to Canada,
- detailed analysis of contemporary Polish society,
- formal assessment of knowledge.

The following areas belong primarily to later modules:

- symbols and identity — Module 02,
- everyday language use — Module 03,
- celebrations and traditions — Module 04,
- music, dance and arts — Module 05,
- food culture — Module 06,
- places, landscapes and travel — Module 07,
- contemporary achievements and life — Module 08.

## Narrative Thread

**We meet Poland for the first time and begin to discover what kind of place it is.**

The program begins with an invitation rather than a definition.

Participants enter a shared journey in which Poland is presented as a place that can be located, observed, heard, discussed and connected with personal experience. They begin with broad questions:

- Where is Poland?
- Who lives there?
- What might everyday life look like?
- How can someone be connected to Poland?
- What would we like to discover next?

The module does not require participants to arrive with prior knowledge, Polish ancestry or Polish-language ability. Every child enters the program as an explorer.

By the end of the module, participants should be ready to ask how Poland can be recognized and how people express relationships with it. This creates the transition to Module 02 — Symbols & Identity.

---

## Big Idea

**Poland is a living and diverse place that people can discover through geography, language, culture, everyday life and personal connections.**

This idea should remain meaningful even after individual facts have been forgotten.

The module should leave participants with the understanding that Poland is more than a location on a map and more than a collection of traditional images. It is a country experienced differently by different people, including:

- people living in Poland,
- Polish communities abroad,
- multilingual and multicultural families,
- people encountering Polish culture for the first time.

---

## Essential Questions

1. **What makes a country more than a place on a map?**
2. **What can we discover about Poland by looking at how people live, speak, create and connect?**
3. **How can people living in Canada have different relationships with Poland?**
4. **What are we curious to learn about Poland?**
5. **How can we explore a culture respectfully when it may or may not be part of our own family story?**

These questions guide future content development. They should not be converted into a fixed quiz or checklist.

## Learning Outcomes

### Knowledge

By the end of the module, participants should be able to:

- identify Poland as a country in Europe,
- identify Warsaw as the capital of Poland,
- recognize that Poland includes cities, towns, villages and varied natural environments,
- recognize that Polish is a language used in Poland and in communities outside Poland,
- identify at least one way in which Poland and Canada are connected through people, families or communities.

### Understanding

Participants should be able to:

- explain in age-appropriate language that a country includes people, places, languages, stories and everyday experiences,
- describe Poland as both historical and contemporary,
- recognize that people may have strong, limited, indirect or newly developing connections with Poland,
- understand that there is no single experience that represents every Polish person or family,
- distinguish between beginning to explore Poland and attempting to describe the whole country.

### Language

Depending on language level, participants should be able to:

- recognize or use selected Polish words connected with greeting, place and belonging,
- understand that Polish words may appear alongside English to support participation and discovery,
- use one or more simple expressions to greet someone or identify Poland,
- connect selected Polish words with meaningful concepts rather than memorize them in isolation.

### Skills

Participants should be able to:

- locate Poland in relation to Europe and Canada using an age-appropriate visual reference,
- observe and interpret selected visual, spoken or environmental information,
- ask questions about Poland,
- compare selected aspects of life in Poland and Canada without ranking one country above the other,
- communicate a personal observation, question or connection.

### Attitudes and Engagement

The module should encourage participants to:

- approach Poland with curiosity,
- feel welcome regardless of language ability or family background,
- recognize different cultural experiences as valid,
- show respect when discussing identity and family connections,
- demonstrate interest in continuing through the program.

---

## Indicators of Successful Engagement

Successful engagement may be visible when a participant:

- identifies or points to Poland on an appropriate map or visual reference,
- recognizes Poland as a country in Europe,
- recognizes or uses at least one selected Polish word,
- responds to a Polish word through speech, gesture, matching or another accessible form,
- makes an observation about Poland,
- asks a question about Poland,
- identifies one selected connection between Poland and Canada,
- notices that people may relate to Poland in different ways,
- participates without being required to disclose ancestry or family history,
- shows interest in another part of the program,
- expresses curiosity about how Poland can be recognized,
- demonstrates readiness to continue to Module 02.

These indicators are not tests, assessment criteria, scoring categories or formal achievement requirements.

A participant does not need to demonstrate every indicator. Engagement may be expressed verbally, visually, physically, collaboratively or through attentive observation.

## Core Topics

### Essential Content

#### 1. Poland as a Place

- Poland is a country in Europe.
- Poland can be located in relation to Canada and other familiar places.
- Warsaw is the capital.
- Poland contains different kinds of communities and environments.

#### 2. Poland as a Place Where People Live

- Poland is experienced through ordinary life as well as public culture.
- Children and families in Poland learn, work, travel, communicate, celebrate and spend time together.
- Daily life is not identical for everyone.

#### 3. First Encounters With Polish Language

- Polish is a living language used for communication.
- A small number of meaningful words can help participants enter the program.
- English supports access to essential meaning.

#### 4. Connections With Canada

- People, families and communities create connections between Poland and Canada.
- Polish communities form part of Canada’s multicultural environment.
- Connections may be maintained through family, language, culture, community participation or personal interest.

#### 5. Curiosity and Inquiry

- Participants may begin with different levels of prior knowledge.
- Questions are a valid starting point for learning.
- The module should help participants identify what they want to discover next.

### Optional Extensions

Optional future development may include:

- selected contemporary images of everyday life,
- age-appropriate references to travel distance or time zones,
- introductory recognition of selected landscapes,
- short personal stories representing different connections with Poland,
- introductory references to Polish communities in Canada,
- examples of Polish words present in local community environments.

Optional extensions must not overwhelm the introductory function of the module or duplicate the core responsibility of later modules.

## Cultural Connections

This module introduces culture through broad points of entry rather than through a catalogue of heritage objects.

Relevant cultural connections include:

- Polish as a living language,
- contemporary and historical layers of place,
- family and community relationships,
- connections across borders,
- ordinary life in Poland,
- the diversity of communities and experiences,
- curiosity about how people understand and express their relationships with Poland.

Future materials should not introduce Poland primarily through folk costumes, food, castles, red-and-white decoration, famous historical figures, rural landscapes or hardship, war and migration narratives.

These elements may appear in appropriate parts of the wider program, but they should not become substitutes for the complexity of the country and its people.

Poland should not be presented as culturally uniform, frozen in the past, defined only by tradition, experienced identically by every family or meaningful only to participants of Polish ancestry.

Where traditional elements are referenced, they should be placed within a broader context that also recognizes contemporary life.

---

## Canada Connections

Canada is the lived context through which many participants will approach Poland.

### Multicultural Context

Folklorama provides a setting in which participants encounter cultures through curiosity, participation and exchange.

Module 01 should reinforce that learning about Poland forms part of learning how people and communities contribute to multicultural Canada.

### Polish Communities in Canada

At an introductory level, the module may acknowledge that:

- people with connections to Poland live in Canada,
- individuals and families maintain those connections in different ways,
- some families use Polish regularly and others do not,
- cultural participation may continue when language knowledge is limited,
- community organizations and events can create new relationships with Polish culture.

This module does not provide a detailed history of migration or Polish communities in Canada.

### Similarities and Differences

Future materials may support broad, age-appropriate observations such as:

- both countries include large cities and smaller communities,
- both countries include varied landscapes and seasons,
- families in both countries may use more than one language,
- experiences of school, home and community may be similar in some ways and different in others.

Comparisons should remain exploratory. They should not be used to determine which country is better, more advanced or more authentic.

### Participants Without Polish Heritage

The module must communicate that:

- no Polish background is required,
- no participant needs to explain their ancestry,
- learning a Polish word or asking a question is meaningful participation,
- cultural exploration is open to everyone,
- respectful curiosity is more important than prior knowledge.

## Suggested Learning Experiences

This section defines categories of future educational experiences. It does not prescribe exercises, instructions or lesson sequences.

### Observation

Participants encounter appropriate maps, images, words, sounds or environmental elements that establish Poland as a real and contemporary place.

### Orientation

Participants develop a basic sense of where Poland is and how it relates geographically to Europe and Canada.

### Conversation

Participants share observations, questions and optional personal connections without being required to disclose family history.

### Storytelling

Short narratives may present different ways people encounter, remember, visit or connect with Poland.

### Comparison

Participants notice selected similarities and differences between life in Poland and Canada.

### Language Encounter

Participants hear, recognize or use a small number of Polish words in meaningful contexts.

### Collaborative Discovery

Participants develop understanding together by noticing, questioning and combining information.

### Personal Meaning-Making

Participants identify what interests them and what they would like to explore later in the program.

### Family Connection

Optional experiences may invite families to discuss connections, memories or questions while remaining fully accessible to families without Polish heritage.

## Age Differentiation

Age differentiation should change complexity, communication, independence and expected reasoning. It should not create three unrelated versions of the module.

### Ages 5–7

**Developmental emphasis:** Concrete, visual, sensory and relational entry points; recognizing Poland as a place; locating it with substantial visual support; noticing people, homes, schools and landscapes; hearing and optionally trying selected Polish words; expressing curiosity through short verbal or non-verbal responses.

**Communication:** Short direct statements, one main idea at a time, visual support, limited abstraction and no reliance on reading ability.

**Expected independence:** Adult modelling, repetition, supported choices and multiple response modes may be required.

**Conceptual level:** Poland can be introduced as a country where different people live. Complex concepts should use concrete examples rather than formal definitions.

### Ages 8–10

**Developmental emphasis:** Organizing information, making comparisons and explaining simple relationships; locating Poland using more than one reference point; identifying everyday life; comparing selected experiences; recognizing varied relationships with Poland; developing questions.

**Communication:** Concise explanations with meaningful detail, selected cultural and geographical vocabulary, complete but manageable statements and visual context.

**Expected independence:** Children should be able to work with simple information, make supported choices and contribute to shared understanding.

**Conceptual level:** Culture and community may be discussed as ideas when connected to concrete examples.

### Ages 11–13

**Developmental emphasis:** Interpreting different representations of Poland; considering how geography, language, migration and community shape relationships; identifying stereotypes; comparing perspectives; generating substantive questions.

**Communication:** Accurate and age-respectful terminology, room for ambiguity, avoidance of oversimplified slogans and contexts relevant to young people.

**Expected independence:** Participants should interpret information, explain reasoning and connect ideas.

**Conceptual level:** Identity, heritage, representation and multiculturalism may be introduced while remaining grounded in the module’s introductory purpose.

---

## Language Differentiation

Language differentiation provides different points of entry into the same conceptual module. Language proficiency must not determine access to cultural understanding.

### No Polish

Participants with no knowledge of Polish should be able to engage fully with every central idea.

The module should provide English access to all essential meaning, present Polish words as discoveries rather than tests, use pronunciation support where appropriate, allow recognition before spoken production, avoid compulsory public repetition and make successful participation possible without speaking Polish.

### Beginner

Support recognition of repeated words, connections between words and people, places or actions, basic greetings, simple identification of Poland and short supported responses.

### Intermediate

Support short descriptions, simple questions and answers, selected Poland–Canada comparisons, personal observations and contextual vocabulary use.

### Advanced

Support precise geographical and cultural vocabulary, explanation of different relationships with Poland, interpretation of representations, discussion of stereotypes and connected Polish expression.

Advanced speakers should not automatically be assigned the role of translator, helper or representative of Polish culture.

## Connections to Other Modules

### Connection From the Educational Framework and Program Architecture

Module 01 translates the broad goal of discovering Poland into an accessible starting point.

It establishes the learner-centred, bilingual and culturally connected approach that should remain consistent throughout the program.

### Connection to Module 02 — Symbols & Identity

Module 01 asks:

- What is Poland?
- Where is it?
- Who may feel connected to it?
- What would we like to discover?

Module 02 will develop the next questions:

- How do people recognize Poland?
- Which symbols are official, cultural or personal?
- How can symbols communicate identity?
- Can different people interpret the same symbol differently?

Module 01 introduces the idea of connection but does not provide an extended treatment of flags, emblems, colours, national symbols or identity.

### Connections to Later Modules

- **Module 03 — Everyday Polish:** develops first language encounters into purposeful communication.
- **Module 04 — Celebrations & Traditions:** explores selected ways people mark meaningful occasions.
- **Module 05 — Music, Dance & Arts:** expands cultural discovery through creative expression.
- **Module 06 — Taste of Poland:** introduces food as a sensory, social and cultural experience.
- **Module 07 — Explore Poland:** develops initial geographical orientation into a broader journey through places and landscapes.
- **Module 08 — Poland Today:** expands the contemporary dimension introduced in Module 01.

### Recurring Concepts

Concepts that may return include Polska / Poland, odkrywanie / discovery, people and community, language, place, connection, diversity and tradition and contemporary life.

Later modules should deepen these concepts rather than repeat the same introductory content.

## Accuracy and Verification Requirements

All future content developed from this specification must distinguish between stable general concepts, facts requiring authoritative confirmation, contemporary information that may change and interpretations requiring cultural or historical judgment.

### Geographic Information

Verify maps, borders, names and status of cities, descriptions of physical geography, distances, travel information, time zones, neighbouring countries and administrative divisions using current authoritative sources.

### Contemporary Information

Review demographic information, population data, public institutions, language-use patterns, contemporary communities, social or cultural statistics and descriptions of everyday life near publication.

### Language Claims

Verify claims about who speaks Polish, where it is used, multilingualism, regional language use, minority languages, forms of address, pronunciation, translation equivalence and social context. Precision language guidance should be reviewed by a qualified specialist where needed.

### Demographic and Social Claims

Use credible sources, avoid unsupported generalizations, distinguish examples from population-wide patterns, recognize regional, generational and individual differences and avoid treating one family or community as representative.

### Poland–Canada Migration History

Historical claims require reliable sources, clear time frames, careful terminology, distinction between migration periods and avoidance of one simplified narrative. Detailed migration history remains outside Module 01 scope.

### Regional, Ethnic, Religious and Linguistic Communities

Avoid cultural uniformity and token representation, use accepted terminology, verify descriptions, distinguish self-identification from external classification and avoid implying that one community represents all of Poland.

### Source Requirements

Preferred sources include official public institutions, recognized educational and cultural institutions, authoritative geographic sources, professionally edited historical research, established language references and reputable Canadian and Polish archival or community sources.

Promotional, unsourced or informal online content is insufficient for substantive claims.

### Cultural or Historical Consultation

Consultation should be considered when content presents a community’s identity or experience, addresses migration, war, displacement or political history, describes religious or ethnic traditions, interprets symbols or practices, represents contested history, makes claims about contemporary social experience or could reinforce stereotypes.

Consultation does not replace factual sourcing.

## Design Notes

### Design Priorities

Future authors should begin with curiosity rather than facts, present Poland through people and lived experience, maintain full access through English, use Polish as a meaningful invitation, represent contemporary life and historical continuity, include different relationships with Poland, preserve the introductory function and create a clear transition to Module 02.

### Content Discipline

A topic belongs in Module 01 only when it helps participants locate Poland, understand it as a living place, recognize different relationships, begin asking questions and prepare for the rest of the program.

Attractive but non-essential content should be reserved for the module that owns it.

### Risks to Avoid

- encyclopedic overload,
- heritage assumptions,
- token bilingualism,
- folklorization,
- historical fixation,
- false uniformity,
- comparison as competition,
- excessive abstraction,
- premature development of later modules.

### Brand Alignment

The module should express eKanada as welcoming, competent, responsible, contemporary, supportive and trustworthy.

It should prioritize the learner, connect language with culture, remain accessible to contemporary families and encourage continued participation.
